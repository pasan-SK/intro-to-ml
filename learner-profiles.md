title: 'Learner Profiles'
---

This workshop is aimed at life scientists, clinicians, and clinical researchers
who are comfortable with data analysis but new to predictive modelling. The
profiles below describe the kind of learner we had in mind when designing it.

## Profile 1: The clinical researcher

Runs t-tests and ANOVAs regularly to compare patient groups, and is confident
reading a results table. Has never trained a predictive model, and isn't sure
what "machine learning" would actually add beyond the statistics they already
know. Comfortable with basic Python and pandas: can load a CSV and inspect a
DataFrame, but hasn't built anything beyond that.

**What they want from this workshop:** a clear sense of *when* predictive
modelling is the right tool instead of a hypothesis test, and hands-on
experience with the real scikit-learn workflow so the next paper that uses
ML doesn't feel like a black box.

## Profile 2: The life scientist working with tabular assay data

Regularly produces tabular data from biomarker panels, assays, or imaging
measurements, and is used to summarising it descriptively. Has heard
colleagues mention "AI" or "machine learning" models being applied to similar
data and is curious whether the same approach could apply to their own work,
but has no formal background in it.

**What they want from this workshop:** to leave with a working mental model of
the full pipeline, from raw measurements to an interpretable result, that they
could realistically adapt to their own dataset afterwards.

## What this workshop assumes

- Basic Python syntax (variables, lists, dictionaries).
- Basic pandas operations (loading a CSV, viewing a DataFrame).
- No prior machine learning or statistics beyond introductory level.
